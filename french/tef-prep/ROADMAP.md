# TEF Canada Preparation — Program Roadmap

Owner/methodologist: ClarkTheMethodologist
Status: living document — update as phases complete or plan changes
Audience: this file is the reference other agents implementing curriculum pieces should read first

## 1. Goal

Take the learner from **zero French knowledge** to **TEF Canada exam-ready at NCLC 7+ (≈ CEFR B2)** across all four skills. NCLC 7 is the practical target for immigration point-scoring purposes — a bare pass is not the goal, a strong, consistent B2 is.

## 2. Test format (what we're training for)

TEF Canada scores four independent sections — a learner can be strong in one and weak in another, so each needs its own track:

| Section | Skill | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Compréhension écrite | Reading | ~40 min, MCQ on texts of increasing difficulty |
| Compréhension orale | Listening | ~40 min, MCQ on audio (announcements, dialogues, news) |
| Expression écrite | Writing | ~60 min, 2 tasks: Section A (short factual message/letter), Section B (argumentative essay, take a position) |
| Expression orale | Speaking | ~15 min, 2 tasks: Section A (obtain info via questions, role-play), Section B (present and defend an opinion) |

Notes for training design:
- TEF Canada listening/speaking materials skew toward **Québécois/Canadian French** — accent, register, and idiom exposure should reflect that, not just Metropolitan French.
- Writing and speaking are the two sections with no existing tooling anywhere yet — they are the highest-priority gaps (see §6).

## 3. Target level and effort estimate

- Target: NCLC 7 (B2) minimum on all four sections, higher where achievable.
- From zero, CEFR benchmarks suggest roughly **600–700 cumulative study hours** to reach B2:
  - A1: ~80h
  - A2: +~150h
  - B1: +~200h
  - B2: +~200h
- **Learner commitment: 1–2h/day (confirmed).** At an average of 1.5h/day (~10.5h/week), that puts the full roadmap at roughly **13–15 months** of consistent study. Low end (1h/day) stretches to ~20 months; high end (2h/day) compresses to ~10–11 months. Phase-by-phase at 1.5h/day:

  | Phase | Hours | ~Duration @1.5h/day |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 — Foundations (A1) | ~80h | ~7–8 weeks |
  | 2 — Elementary (A2) | ~150h | ~14 weeks |
  | 3 — Intermediate (B1) | ~200h | ~19 weeks |
  | 4 — Exam-ready (B2) | ~200h | ~19 weeks |

  Actual pace should be recalibrated against real progress at each phase exit, not assumed up front — this is a planning estimate, not a deadline.

- **No target test date — optimizing for speed at own pace.** With no external deadline, the lever that matters most is *consistency*, not calendar pressure: daily practice beats sporadic longer sessions, since spaced repetition (vocab) and skill-building (speaking/writing) both degrade with gaps. Practical implications:
  - Lean toward the top of the 1–2h/day range (i.e. 2h) on days it's sustainable — this is what compresses the timeline from ~15 months toward ~10–11 months — but prioritize *not skipping days* over occasionally going long.
  - Phase transitions should be triggered by hitting exit criteria (§4), not by a schedule — if a phase goes faster than estimated, move on immediately rather than padding it out.
  - Mock exams (§5, track 7) should start early and run frequently — they're the fastest way to catch a phase that's taking longer than expected and reallocate effort, which matters more than the hours-estimate table above when going at max sustainable speed.

### Daily session allocation (1–2h/day)

With only 1–2h/day and two currently-untooled tracks (speaking, writing — see §6), sessions need to be split deliberately so vocabulary/grammar don't crowd out the harder-to-practice skills:

- ~30% vocabulary + grammar drilling (can overlap, e.g. Anki-style review)
- ~25% listening/reading comprehension
- ~25% speaking practice
- ~20% writing practice

Ratio should shift toward speaking/writing as phases progress — early on (Phase 1) foundations dominate by necessity, but by Phase 3–4 speaking and writing should get the largest share since they're both the exam's hardest sections and the least naturally reinforced by passive study.

## 4. Phase roadmap

### Phase 1 — Foundations (CEFR A1)
- Grammar: être/avoir/aller/faire, present tense, articles, gender & number agreement, basic negation, basic question forms.
- Vocabulary: ~500–800 highest-frequency words (numbers, time, family, food, daily routine, basic verbs).
- Phonetics: French sound system, nasal vowels, liaison, stress/rhythm patterns — Québécois pronunciation specifics called out explicitly, not left implicit.
- Listening/Reading: isolated words and short fixed phrases only.
- Exit criteria: can produce simple present-tense sentences unaided, recognize the ~500-word core set by ear and in print.

### Phase 2 — Elementary (CEFR A2)
- Grammar: passé composé, imparfait, futur proche/simple, comparatives, reflexive verbs, basic pronouns (y, en, direct/indirect object).
- Vocabulary: grows to ~1500–2000 words, including everyday functional language (shopping, appointments, directions, small talk).
- Listening/Reading: short authentic material (ads, simple articles, dialogues) begins.
- Speaking/Writing: first guided, non-exam-format production (short scripted exchanges, simple paragraphs).
- Exit criteria: can narrate a past event, follow a slow simple conversation, write a short coherent paragraph.

### Phase 3 — Intermediate (CEFR B1)
- Grammar: subjunctive (present, common triggers), conditional, relative clauses, more complex connectors (bien que, afin que, etc.).
- Vocabulary: opinion/argument vocabulary, abstract topics, register awareness (formal vs. informal).
- Listening/Reading: TEF-style passages at reduced difficulty; note-taking practice.
- Speaking/Writing: first **timed** drills, still below exam length/complexity.
- Exit criteria: can state and support an opinion, understand native-paced speech on familiar topics, handle unscripted short conversation.

### Phase 4 — Exam-ready (CEFR B2 / TEF-specific)
- Grammar: consolidation only — no new major structures, focus on accuracy under time pressure.
- Vocabulary: exam-register vocabulary (formal letters, argumentative essays), idiom/register flexibility for listening.
- Full TEF-format drilling:
  - Writing: Section A (factual/formal message) and Section B (200+ word argumentative essay), timed, scored against TEF criteria.
  - Speaking: Section A (info-gathering role-play) and Section B (opinion defense), timed, with feedback.
  - Reading/Listening: full-length timed mock sections.
- Full mock exams under real conditions (timing, format, no aids) at regular intervals, not just at the end.
- Exit criteria: consistent B2+ performance across ≥3 consecutive full mock exams, sat close enough together to trust the trend.

## 5. Skill tracks (what future agents own)

Each track is a standing responsibility, active across all phases at the appropriate difficulty — not a one-time task:

1. **Vocabulary** — spaced-repetition word acquisition, leveled by phase, tagged by topic/register.
2. **Grammar** — structured progression of grammar points with drills, matched to the phase roadmap in §4.
3. **Listening** — leveled audio comprehension, shifting toward Québécois accent/register as phases progress, eventually TEF-format mock listening sections.
4. **Reading** — leveled text comprehension, eventually TEF-format mock reading sections.
5. **Speaking** — currently the biggest gap. Needs interactive dialogue practice and pronunciation/fluency feedback, progressing to timed TEF Section A/B speaking tasks with evaluation against TEF criteria.
6. **Writing** — also a major gap. Needs guided composition with correction, progressing to timed TEF Section A/B writing tasks scored against TEF criteria (task achievement, coherence, vocabulary range, grammatical accuracy).
7. **Mock exam / assessment** — periodic full-length, timed, real-condition mock exams across all four sections, used to check phase exit criteria and recalibrate the timeline in §3.

## 6. Priority gaps (build these first)

- **Speaking track**: no tooling exists yet anywhere. Highest priority — speaking is hardest to practice without dedicated support.
- *Partial progress:* `reading-trainer.html` + `reading-exercises/` give Phase 1 read-aloud practice on phonetics (so far: nasal vowels [ɛ̃] [ɑ̃] [ɔ̃], and closed [e] vs. open [ɛ]). The learner reads a passage with the target sounds highlighted, then compares against TTS. There's still no feedback on the learner's own pronunciation, so this gap stays open.
- **Writing track with correction/scoring**: no tooling exists yet. Second priority.
- **Leveling**: existing vocabulary/grammar material (if any, elsewhere in this repo) is not phase-tagged; new material produced against this roadmap should be tagged by phase (1–4) from the start so progress is measurable against §4's exit criteria.
- **Mock exam track**: should not be built last-minute — needs to exist in a lightweight form early so we get real calibration data on pace, not just at the end of Phase 4.
